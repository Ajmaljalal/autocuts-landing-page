Autocuts vs QuickTime Player

QuickTime Player is the free recorder built into every Mac. Autocuts is the recorder you upgrade to when the video has an audience. The difference is everything that happens after you press stop.

Short verdict

QuickTime Player proves a Mac can record its screen for free, and for a private capture it is enough. The moment a recording has viewers, Autocuts is the better tool: it zooms into your clicks, smooths the cursor, composites the camera in ten layouts, adds captions, and exports 4K, all for a one-time $19.99 license. You can record with Autocuts free before you buy, so the upgrade costs nothing to test.

Where the products overlap

Native Mac apps

Both run natively on the Mac and record without a browser, an account, or an upload.

Free to start

QuickTime is free. Autocuts lets you record and keep a video before buying a license.

Local files

Both produce a normal local video file that you can store, edit, or publish anywhere.

Can QuickTime Player record system audio?

Not on its own. The built-in screen recording captures the microphone, but the Mac’s own output needs a third-party audio-routing driver. Autocuts records system audio alongside the microphone and camera on supported macOS versions, which matters for app demos with sound.

Do I need Autocuts if QuickTime is free?

For a private capture that nobody else watches, QuickTime is enough. Every video with an audience earns its keep in Autocuts: it generates zooms from your clicks, smooths the cursor, frames the recording on a background, adds captions, and exports launch-ready 4K. One tutorial that looks professional pays back the $19.99 license.

Which is better for tutorials?

A tutorial lives or dies on whether viewers can follow the pointer and read the interface. QuickTime records exactly what happened, at desktop scale. Autocuts zooms into each click and redraws cursor motion, so the same take reads clearly even on a phone screen.
