Autocuts vs Screen Charm

Both are one-time-purchase Mac screen recorders that add automatic zoom and smooth cursor motion to a recording. They differ in captions, prompting, sharing, export formats, and price, and Autocuts costs less than half as much.

Short verdict

Both apps charge once and never again, but Autocuts costs $19.99 while Screen Charm costs $79, nearly four times as much. For the lower price, Autocuts adds on-device captions with transcript cleanup, a voice-paced teleprompter, and WebM plus subtitle-file export. Screen Charm's extras are motion blur and a music library, while Autocuts matches its hosted links with free shareable links for life. Unless one of those extras is essential, Autocuts is the better buy, and you can record with it before paying anything.

Where the products overlap

One-time pricing

Neither product is a subscription. Both sell a license once, with no monthly fee.

Automatic motion

Both generate zooms around clicks and smooth the cursor so a desktop workflow is easier to follow.

Native Apple Silicon apps

Both are Mac-only recorders built for Apple Silicon that record screen, webcam, microphone, and system audio.

Autocuts delivers the capture, the automatic zoom, the smooth cursor, captions, and the teleprompter for $19.99. Why Autocuts is the better buy

Autocuts records speech into an on-device transcript, so you cut filler words from text, burn in captions, and export SRT and VTT files without uploading anything. The teleprompter paces itself from your voice instead of a scroll timer. Export covers WebM as well as MP4. You keep $59, and you can record and keep a video before buying.

How do the prices compare?

Both are one-time purchases with no subscription, so the comparison is clean: Screen Charm lists $79, and Autocuts lists $19.99, about a quarter of the price. The Autocuts license covers one person on up to two Macs, with lifetime updates and a 14-day refund window.
