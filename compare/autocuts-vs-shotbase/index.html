Autocuts vs Shotbase

Both are modern Mac capture tools with automatic zoom and editing. Autocuts specializes in narrated video from record to export; Shotbase covers screenshots, webpages, recordings, organization, editing, 3D presentation, and sharing.

Short verdict

Choose Shotbase if you want one searchable library for screenshots, scrolling or responsive webpage captures, recordings, and shared assets. Choose Autocuts if your main job is a polished product demo or tutorial with camera, system audio, a teleprompter, transcript cleanup, captions, subtitle files, and editable motion graphics.

Autocuts follows the spoken video workflow

The recorder captures screen, camera, microphone, system audio, clicks, cursor points, keys, and voice. The editor uses that material for zoom, cursor, captions, transcript cuts, layouts, and motion graphics.

Shotbase follows the capture-library workflow

Shotbase brings still images, webpage captures, and recordings into one organized Mac workspace. It provides annotation and presentation tools, automatic tags and summaries, search, 3D effects, and share links.

What is the main difference between Autocuts and Shotbase?

Autocuts is focused on narrated screen videos and their complete edit. Shotbase combines screenshots, screen recordings, webpage captures, organization, editing, 3D presentation, and sharing in one Mac library.

Which is better for tutorial and product-demo videos?

Autocuts is the more focused choice when you need camera and system-audio capture, a teleprompter, transcript cleanup, captions, subtitle files, and motion graphics. Manual placement works directly. Generated suggestions require a transcript and a user-installed Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, or Cursor CLI tool. Shotbase is stronger when screenshots and organized capture assets are equally important.
