Autocuts vs Screen Studio

Both Mac apps turn screen recordings into polished videos with automatic zoom and cursor motion. The difference that grows every month is the bill: Screen Studio renews at $9 to $29 per month, and Autocuts is $34.99 once.

Short verdict

Autocuts delivers the core Screen Studio promise, automatic click zoom and smooth cursor motion on a polished background, for a $34.99 one-time license instead of a subscription. It adds a voice-paced teleprompter and on-device transcript cleanup, and you can record and keep a real video before paying. Screen Studio has a longer feature list today, including hosted links and iPhone capture, but its yearly plan bills $108 up front and $324 over three years. On an Apple Silicon Mac, Autocuts is the better buy.

Where the products overlap

Automatic motion

Both tools can create zooms around clicks and redraw cursor movement so a desktop workflow is easier to follow.

Post-recording design

Both let you change framing, backgrounds, aspect ratio, camera treatment, trims, and speed after recording.

Local transcription

Both describe on-device speech transcription. This avoids uploading the recording solely to create captions.

Autocuts owns the job that Screen Studio rents out

Autocuts covers the work that makes a demo watchable, automatic zoom, cursor motion, captions, and a teleprompter, for one $34.99 payment. Screen Studio wraps similar recording tools in a subscription and adds extras such as iPhone capture, background music, and Intel Mac support. Those extras are what the $9 to $29 per month keeps paying for, every month. If your videos land on a website, in docs, or on YouTube, the extras sit unused while the bill continues, and four months of that bill already pass the lifetime price of Autocuts.

Why Autocuts is the better buy

Autocuts covers the workflow that sells software: record a demo, let automatic zoom and the reconstructed cursor make it watchable, cut filler words from the on-device transcript, and export 4K with subtitle files. It adds a teleprompter that follows your voice, local project files that never upload, and a license that covers one person on up to two Macs with lifetime updates and a 14-day refund window. Record first, and pay only when you are ready to export.

The three-year math

Three years of Screen Studio cost $324 on the yearly plan or $1,044 month to month. Three years of Autocuts cost the same $34.99 you paid on day one. The subscription buys mobile capture and background music; Autocuts includes free shareable links for life at no extra cost.

Which app has better automatic zoom?

Both generate zooms from clicks and allow manual changes. The better result depends on the specific product path, how each app groups nearby actions, and how much control you want over the final timing. The useful test is to record the same two-minute workflow and compare the unedited result. Autocuts makes that test free: record before you buy and keep the video.
