Autocuts vs Loom

Loom is a cloud video messaging tool built around instant share links. Autocuts is a local Mac recorder that turns your clicks into an edited video. The right pick depends on whether the recording is a quick message or a finished asset.

Short verdict

Choose Loom if your team needs instant hosted links and quick async updates across Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android. Choose Autocuts if you record product demos or tutorials on a Mac and want automatic click zooms, a smooth cursor, on-device captions, 4K local export, and a one-time price.

Where the products overlap

Screen and camera capture

Both tools record the screen together with the webcam and microphone for walkthroughs with a presenter.

Captions

Both can produce captions. Autocuts transcribes on your Mac; Loom generates transcripts in its cloud.

Trim editing

Both let you cut mistakes and dead time from a recording before anyone watches it.

When Loom is the stronger choice

Loom is built for speed of delivery inside a team. Recording, hosting, and the share link are one motion, viewers comment in the browser, and the apps cover Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android. If your videos are short internal updates that expire in relevance within a week, Loom fits that job well.

When Autocuts may fit better

Autocuts is built for videos that represent a product: demos, tutorials, and launch clips. It generates zooms from your clicks, redraws the cursor, adds camera layouts and backgrounds, and exports 4K MP4 or WebM with subtitle files. Recordings stay on your Mac, there is no account, and the license is a one-time $34.99 purchase instead of a per-user subscription.

Is Loom free?

Loom has a free Starter plan limited to 25 videos per person, 5 minutes per video, and 720p. Longer or sharper videos need a Business plan at $18 per user per month. Autocuts lets you record and keep a video for free; processing and export unlock with the one-time license.

Can Autocuts share a link like Loom?

Not today. Autocuts produces a local video file, so you host it wherever it will live: a landing page, YouTube, documentation, or a customer message. If instant hosted links are the core of your workflow, Loom is the better match.
