Autocuts vs ScreenFlow

Both record and edit on a Mac. Autocuts is built to draft a polished software walkthrough automatically; ScreenFlow is a deeper general editor for people who want detailed multi-track production control.

Short verdict

Choose ScreenFlow when you need iPhone or iPad capture, a stock-media library, layered projects, and detailed text or video animation. Choose Autocuts when you want automatic click zooms, cursor reconstruction, transcript cleanup, captions, and motion graphics in a faster screen-first workflow at a much lower one-time price.

Autocuts is optimized for explaining software quickly

Clicks, cursor movement, keystrokes, and narration remain useful data after recording. The editor turns them into zooms, smoother cursor motion, captions, transcript cuts, and optional motion-graphic suggestions.

ScreenFlow is built for deeper manual production

ScreenFlow provides multi-track media management, styles and templates, stock media access, iOS recording, text and video animation, multiple publishing options, and professional export choices. Those tools suit larger, layered projects.

About these facts

ScreenFlow features and its $199 starting price were checked against the official ScreenFlow overview on August 26, 2026. Prices and features can change.

What is the main difference between Autocuts and ScreenFlow?

Autocuts is a focused automatic screen recorder and editor for Apple Silicon Macs. ScreenFlow is a broader professional Mac editor with multi-track media management, iOS capture, stock media, templates, and detailed animation tools.

Which costs less?

As checked August 26, 2026, Autocuts costs $19.99 once and ScreenFlow lists a starting price of $199. Prices can change, so confirm them on each official site before buying.

How does Autocuts generate motion graphics?

You can place lower thirds, stats, callouts, quotes, lists, and highlights manually. Generated suggestions require a transcript and a user-installed Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, or Cursor CLI tool.
